About a month ago, I started building a shed—a project I thought would be easily completed in a few weeks with whatever amount of spare time I could throw at it. I thought with the help of a few friends and time in the evening I could have this shed built before I knew it. Yet a month later, it stands incomplete. The soffit needs to be finished, the vinyl siding needs to be added, a door needs to be hung, and the plexiglass for windows needs to cut and installed. And while, as far as shed building is concerned, I am certainly on the up and up, this last bit of work will take at least a dozen hours. Which means, I am still in the thick of it.

But in the thick of what exactly? The thick of all the negative emotions this shed building has conjured. The sense of fear I get from doing so many things wrong. The gripping anxiety I experience from spending hundreds of dollars at a time at hardware stores. The fact that I feel like a looser for sacrificing so much time with my wife. The culling of time spent on other personal interests I find significantly more appealing and important. The crushing sense that this thing will never be finished.  The fear that somehow this thing will get destroyed by a force of nature or as a result of my lack of building skills. The understanding that I will never get back all those moments I spent outside slamming pieces of wood together or checking if things were square. The paralyzing hold this unfinished project has on my mind. The sense of doubt I get when I consider if this shed will be worth the time, money, and effort. 

Honestly, If I had known just how thick the thick of it would be, I would have never broken ground. I would have never started saving reference photos. Instead, I would have bought a plastic kit or a pre-manufactured building right off the lot. But now, those options are off the table, and the thick of it is all that remains. The fear, the paralyzation, the sense of doubt, the unwanted obsession, the sense that I am doing something deeply wrong, the unbalanced equation drawn from trading so much for what feels like so little. 

And now I don’t know what to do except to finish this damn thing, to keep taking one step after another into the shrouded expanse and to write about this experience, to get it all out, to escape the thick of it by introducing the thinness of considerate prose. To do what I love, even though completing that which I have honestly grown to hate requires more of my time and energy.
