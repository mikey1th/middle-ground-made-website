Peniaphobia is the fear of poverty, and for people many, it’s the foundation which their entire life is built upon. Many people work a job they hate, deal with shit they can’t stand, mingle with people they want to avoid, and sacrifice the best years of their lives simply because they are scared of being poor. 

And to be fair, there are countless reasons why being poor sucks. You get tossed around by a system that doesn’t care if you live, die, or starve. Your neighborhood and surrounding environments  are clad in chaos and irrationality. Bugs infest your house. Mold creeps up your walls. Aggressive dogs prowl your streets. Gangs assault and kill each other outside your home. And drugs and paraphernalia litter your parks and sidewalks—if you’re luck enough to have them. 

Yet despite these awful conditions,  poverty is not a death sentence and it’s not the end of joy. I survived being poor and somehow, a few of my fondest memories come from that time. Somewhere between walking to the store and my parents missing payments for the water bill, I experienced real moments of happiness. And the same is true for millions of other people who are currently in poverty. Despite the opinions of the rich, poverty is not synonymous with destitution. Joy, hope, love, connection, community, enjoyable hobbies, good food, rest, and joviality, are all possible even when you’re poor.

Which means that even if we become behind on rent, regularly eat “struggle meals,” or receive a form of government welfare, life remains what we make it—and the best things it has to offer are still on the table. Once we realize that our sense of joy, happiness, and contentment don’t have to be directly tied to where we fall on the income spectrum we can begin to approach life freed from the burden of being tossed into the lowest tax bracket.
