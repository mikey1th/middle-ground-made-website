Of all the home and garden projects I’ve completed, none have been so tedious as building a fence. None have been so attention hungry as simply arranging a bit of wood in a straight line. No project, except for building a shed, has been so devastatingly difficult at every turn. 

But why is building a fence so annoying and difficult and tedious?

The first reason is that a fence is a contradiction. The chief purpose of a fence is to separate this from that—my stuff from your stuff—which is an ugly ordeal, yet we try to make this ugly thing as nice as we can. And unfortunately, this desire to nicely say “stay the hell away from my stuff” condemns the project to an unstable foundation—no matter how deep the post holes are dug.
Second, building a fence is an affront to nature. In building a fence we attempt to divide that which is indivisible, that which envelopes the entire world and universe, and in return it mocks us. Nature sees our feeble attempts at division and says “you can’t separate me from me, and if you try, you’re going to have a bad time.” 

The final reason is that we make the fence run along a 90 degree angle, or a perfectly straight line. Nature seems to take this as an insult she cannot ignore. Not only do we erroneously attempt to divide her and own her, but we do so by erecting a 90 degree line. We divide nature with a technique that is completely foreign to her. 

All of these reasons culminate in nature colluding to thwart our plan. Every pebble is in just the right place to hurt our knees. The sun seems to follow us wherever we move. And conspicuously placed roots and rocks make digging a hole an impossible task. But despite her best tricks we continue on, and eventually finish our contradictive and offensive attempt at division.

But nature is longer winded than even our best built fences and more patient than even the most experienced tradesperson. Eventually, that which we spent so much time building will be torn down not by a crane, or a new home owner, or an electric saw—but by rain, wind, sun, and sleet and in the end nature unite that which was temporarily divided.
