Most of us fall somewhere on the spectrum of peniaphobia. Some can’t imagine being poor without an adverse mind-body reaction, while others regard being poor as an unremarkable un-comfortability. But regardless of where we land, I think the most common solution to peniaphobia is to find a job and to subsequently, do whatever we can to keep it. Unfortunately, “solving” our fear of poverty with a job is a lot like solving arachnophobia with avoiding spiders. While the sense of fear might decrease, the  fear itself remains present as ever. In both cases we avoid that which we fear and never get down to the root of why we were scared in the first place. In fact, our hyper avoidance creates another, more sinister problem. 

If we come to see our job as our salvation from poverty and the sole solution to our peniaphobia we will eventually develop ergophobia, which is the fear of loosing one’s job. And once we develop ergophobia and accept the fake fix, we are well on our way to developing a host of other fears and phobias. Once we become afraid of loosing our jobs what’s to stop us from developing a fear of our boss, a fear of being late, a fear of missing a meeting,  a fear of taking vacation, or any similar work-place related fear.  All of a sudden our peniaphobia, which became ergophobia, has turned into a million phobias that dominate our lives and strangle our imagination. And eventually, we will  find ourselves enslaved not only to our jobs, but our sense of fear and our inability to face it. 

But we don’t have to go own this rabbit hole.  We don’t have to allow our lives to become ruled by a metastasized phobia. We can, in essence, fear less. We can learn to fear only fear itself. We can choose to fear not, because Elsewhere will accompany us. 
And sure, once we get a hold of our fear we might choose to keep our jobs, but we do so after a undergoing a beautiful metamorphosis made possible by realizing that once we fear less, we are able to do, to be so much more.
